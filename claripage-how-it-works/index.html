How ClariPage works

From a photo of a page to flashcards, diagrams and recall practice, and why each piece is built the way it is.

Photograph a page. Get back study material in about a minute. Here is what happens in between, and why each part is built the way it is.

  1. Photograph the pages

    The camera stays open between shots, so a whole topic goes in at once. Up to twelve pages. Handwriting or a printed handout both work.

    You pick the reading level once and it is remembered. There is nothing to type in. No terms to enter by hand.

  2. It reads what is actually there

    Spelling gets fixed, abbreviations get expanded, blank pages get skipped. Long topics are read in pieces and joined back together.

    You also get a read confidence rating. Bad handwriting and bad lighting are real. When a page was hard to read, you are told.

  3. Every point gets labeled

    Your child's handwriting, a printed handout, an addition, a correction, or your own reference material. Five labels, always visible.

    Nothing gets blended together. You can separate your child's work from the machine's without reading a word of it.

  4. It names what was missed

    Every set ends with a short summary of what the page left out. Not a scolding. Something like: this page covered the definitions well and skipped the worked examples.

    Over a semester, that becomes a pattern you can act on.

  5. Diagrams, where a diagram helps

    A labeled process, a comparison, an equation with its parts named. Where a figure would not help, it does not draw one. A list of definitions does not need a picture.

    Diagrams follow the reading level too. Bold and simple for a second grader, detailed for a junior.

  6. Your child studies

    Flashcards with read aloud.

    Learn starts with multiple choice and moves to typing answers from memory.

    Match is a timed pairing game. Two minutes in the car.

    Practice test is ten questions, scored instantly, split by label.

    Review brings back everything due today, twenty cards at a time.

Why it is built this way

The study tools are not arbitrary. Each one is built around something researchers have known about learning for a long time.

Retrieval practice

Pulling a fact out of memory strengthens it far more than reading it again. This is why rereading notes feels productive and mostly is not. Recognition is not the same as recall.

Every study mode in ClariPage asks your child to produce the answer before they see it. Flashcards, Learn, Match and the practice test are all retrieval in different disguises.

Spacing

Reviewing something once, then again a few days later, then a week after that, beats the same total time spent in one sitting. Forgetting a little between sessions is part of what makes it work.

The review queue schedules each card for the moment just before your child would forget it. Cards they find easy come back rarely. Cards they miss come back soon.

Fading the help

Learning goes best when support is high at the start and drops away as the learner gets stronger.

Learn does this on purpose. A card starts as multiple choice, where recognizing the answer is enough. Once your child gets it right consistently, the same card switches to typing the answer from memory, with no options to choose from.

Worked examples

For anything procedural, seeing a complete example worked through is more useful than practicing blind. It is also the thing students most often fail to copy down, because the teacher moves faster than a pencil.

That is why the gap summary calls it out specifically when the examples are what went missing.

Knowing what you do not know

Students are famously bad at judging what they have learned. Material that feels familiar gets skipped, and the gaps stay invisible until the test.

The labels and the two score breakdown exist for this reason. They turn a vague feeling of "I studied" into something specific: this part you had, this part was never on your page.

What it will not do

It will not do the homework. It organizes what was taught and names what is missing. It will not answer the worksheet.

It will not hide what it added. Every added line is labeled, always.

It will not work on anything but schoolwork. Other pages are refused, and nothing is read or generated from them.

It will not pretend to be perfect. Handwriting gets misread sometimes. A diagram is occasionally wrong. That is why the confidence rating and the labels are there, and why checking is a better habit than trusting.
