Our mission is simple: help students build good study habits using the material they already have.

Not someone else's flashcard set. Their own notes, and the handouts their teachers give them.

Why we started

ClariPage began as a tool for our own kids.

We wanted them studying from what their teachers actually taught, and we wanted the habit to stick: review regularly, practice recall, notice what you do not know yet. The material was already sitting in their backpacks. What was missing was a way to turn it into something they could study from without a parent rewriting it by hand every Sunday.

So we built that. Then we kept building, because once you can read the page you can also see what the page is missing, and that turned out to be the more useful half.

Why the labels matter

ClariPage fills in what a page leaves out. Plenty of tools will do that much.

What is different is that it labels every point with where it came from. What your child wrote. What came off a handout. What had to be added. What was wrong and got fixed.

Keeping those separate is the whole product.

It means a student knows which parts rest on their teacher's words. It means a parent can see how much of the lesson got captured, without knowing the subject. And it means nobody has to wonder what the AI made up, because the AI's work is marked.

What we believe

Evidence first

A study tool should show its sources. If we cannot tell you where something came from, we should not be asking your child to memorize it.

Better notes, not fewer notes

The goal is a student who takes better notes next semester, not one who stops bothering because an app will cover for them. So we name what was missed, every time. The note taking habit is worth more than any single set of flashcards.

Studying should never be rationed

Photographing pages costs us money and is limited. Practicing, reviewing and testing are not, and will not be. An app that charges a student to keep studying has its incentives pointed the wrong way.

Write for the student in front of you

A page for a second grader should not read like a page for a junior. Pick the reading level and everything follows it: the notes, the questions, even how busy the diagrams are.

The adult holds the account

Students study on a parent's or teacher's login and never hand over their own details. That was a design decision before it was a legal one.

It should refuse things

It will not answer the worksheet. It will not work on anything that is not schoolwork. A tool used by kids should have things it declines to do.

Say what it cannot do

ClariPage reads handwriting well and still gets it wrong sometimes. It draws diagrams that teach and occasionally draws one that is off. We show a confidence rating and label every addition, because a student who checks is better off than one who trusts.
