How it works

From a photograph of a messy page to notes, diagrams and recall practice, in about a minute.

OverviewHow it worksWhy we built itPrivacyTerms

  1. Photograph the pages

    The camera stays open between shots, so a whole topic goes in one pass rather than one page at a time. Up to twelve pages in a set. Handwriting or a printed handout both work, and ClariPage can tell the difference.

    Set the reading level once — elementary, middle or high — and it's remembered. Naming the class and topic is optional and sharpens what gets added later.

  2. It reads the pages

    Images are sent for processing and discarded. Long sets are read in batches and merged, so a big topic isn't truncated. Spelling is corrected, abbreviations expanded, blank pages ignored.

    You also get a read-confidence rating. Bad handwriting and bad lighting are real, and you should know when a page was hard to read.

  3. Every point gets a label

    This is the part that makes ClariPage different from a summariser. Each line carries its origin: your child's handwriting, a printed handout, an addition, a correction, or your own reference material.

    Nothing is merged into anonymous prose. The seams stay visible on purpose.

  4. It names the gaps

    At the end of every set is a short, plain summary of what the notes left out. Not a scolding — just an honest note that the page covered the definitions well and skipped the worked examples.

    Over a term that's the most useful thing in the app. It's the difference between "revise harder" and "you're not writing down the examples".

  5. Diagrams, where they help

    Where a section benefits from a figure, ClariPage draws one: a labelled process, a comparison, an equation with its parts named. Where a section wouldn't benefit, it doesn't. A list of definitions doesn't need a picture, and a cluttered page is harder to study from.

    Diagrams follow the reading level too. A figure for a seven-year-old is bold and simple; one for a sixteen-year-old can carry real detail.

  6. Then you study, as much as you like

    Learn — adaptive practice. Cards start as multiple choice and switch to typing the answer from memory once you've got them right. Typed answers are marked generously: spelling and word order don't matter, the idea does.

    Flashcards — tap to flip, grade yourself, hear it read aloud.

    Match — a timed pairing game. Good for two minutes in the car.

    Practice test — ten questions, scored instantly, with results split by label so you can see whether lost marks were on your own notes or on material the notes had missed.

    Review queue — everything due today across every set, scheduled so cards return just before they'd be forgotten. Twenty at a time, because a wall of eighty is how people quit.

  7. Your own reference material, optionally

    You can add study pages of your own — a revision guide, formula sheets, pages a teacher shared. ClariPage reads them once and keeps the facts. After that, when a capture touches a topic they cover, it draws on them and labels anything taken from them as reference.

    We keep the facts extracted from your pages, not the images, and never show one family's material to anyone else.

What ClariPage deliberately doesn't do

It doesn't do the homework. It organizes what was taught and names what's missing. It won't answer the worksheet.

It doesn't hide the machine's work. Every added line is labelled. If you want only what your child wrote, you can see exactly that.

It only works on schoolwork. Pages that aren't study material are declined, and nothing is read or generated from them.

It doesn't pretend to be perfect. Handwriting gets misread. A diagram is occasionally wrong. That's why the confidence rating and the labels are there — so a student can check rather than assume.
